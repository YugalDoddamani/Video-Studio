# Story: 2.8 km (60 s, narrated)

## Project details

- **Topic:** `content/topics.md` DS-002 — "Her phone is in her hand, but Find My shows a second device named Me, 2.8 km away, and it is closing."
- **Slug:** `28-km-60s`
- **Target duration:** 60.00 s / 1800 frames
- **Fiction disclosure:** `FICTIONAL STORY`, on every scene
- **Audio:** local mix with voice-over (`audio/build-audio.sh`)
- **Supersedes:** `28-km` (30 s, silent). The shorter cut stays in the repo and is never
  overwritten; this is a different film, not a re-export.

## Why this cut exists

The 30 s version was a puzzle with nobody in it. It showed a dot closing on a dot and asked the
viewer to be frightened by arithmetic. Nobody said who she was, where she was going, or what she
stood to lose — so the impossibility arrived before the stakes, and a stranger scrolling past had
no reason to care.

This cut keeps the map and puts a life underneath it: the same night, the same app, the same rule
that the software is never wrong — but now a woman tells you, in her own voice, what it means.

## Premise

Asha, a night nurse, walks home at eleven. Tonight Find My lists a second device called **Me**,
2.8 km away, and it is retracing her walk home in reverse — the hospital, the bus stop, her
street. It is not chasing her. It is repeating her.

## Characters

- **Asha**, 34 — paediatric nurse, six years on the same walk home. Never seen; only heard.
- **Meera**, her sister — never seen, never heard. Present only as a text.
- The device called **Me** — a phone. It walks her night backwards.

## Structure

### Hook (0–5 s)

Frame 1 is the readout: `2.8 km`, the grey pin labelled **Me**, her blue dot dead centre. Her
voice arrives at 0.4 s — *"My phone says I'm two point eight kilometres away. I'm holding it."*
Situation, impossibility and question in one sentence, over one image.

### Setup (5–11 s)

A wet street, headlights, the sound of walking. *"I'm a nurse. I finish at eleven. Same walk home,
every night."* Now the viewer knows who she is and why she is alone.

### Escalation (11–35 s)

*"Tonight there's a second phone on the map. It's called Me."* → the sheet opens on where it has
been: `Hospital 23:11`, `Bus stop 23:19`, `Ring road now` → *"It's walking my night. Backwards."*
That is the turn: not following her — being her.

### Twist (35–48 s)

She rings her sister. Meera texts back: *"you're at my door. i just let you in."* Someone wearing
her location is inside her sister's flat, and Asha is holding her phone.

### Climax (48–56 s)

The map closes 400 m → 3 m. The pin resolves from **Me** to **ASHA**. Her own dot leaves the map.

### Ending (56–60 s)

She kills the phone. Black. The lock screen lights itself: `Me — 0 m away`.
*"It comes back on by itself."*

## Rules this film keeps

- Frame 1 is the impossible image. No logo, no intro card, no "story time".
- The software is never wrong. Every number is the number the app would really print, and the
  distance only shrinks because the other device is moving.
- Nothing on screen explains the anomaly. The narration states facts; it never theorises.
- No line of narration repeats a caption, and no caption repeats a line of narration.
