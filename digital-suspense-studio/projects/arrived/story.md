# Story: Arrived

## Project details

- **Topic:** `content/topics.md` DS-002 — a device on the map that is not hers.
- **Slug:** `arrived`
- **Target duration:** 60.00 s / 1800 frames
- **Fiction disclosure:** `FICTIONAL STORY` on every scene.
- **Audio:** local mix with voice-over (`audio/build-audio.sh`).
- **Supersedes:** `28-km` (30 s, silent) and `28-km-60s` (60 s, narrated). Neither is
  overwritten or deleted; both stay in the repo as earlier attempts at the same bones.

## Why this cut exists

The 60 s version narrated a film the picture was not showing. The voice talked about a sister,
about a night walked backwards, about a name resolving on a pin — while the screen showed two dots
moving on a grid. A viewer arriving cold had no idea who the woman was, where she was going, or
what she stood to lose, and the narration kept describing things that were not in the frame.

This cut is built the other way round. Every image is chosen because it can be read without
sound; every line of narration names something that is visible at that second. If the voice were
removed, the film would still be a story. If the picture were removed, the voice would still
describe things that happened.

It is also one episode. No part two, no hook for a sequel, no thread left hanging to be picked up
later: the last frame is the end of the story.

## Premise

Asha is a night nurse. Her flat is 2.8 km away and she is walking to it. At 23:12 her phone tells
her she arrived at 23:11 — and Find My agrees with it: a second device, called **Me**, is sitting
in her flat with her name on it. Then it leaves, and it comes down her street.

## Characters

- **Asha**, 34 — night nurse, six years of the same walk. Never seen, only heard.
- The device called **Me** — a phone with her name that walks out of her flat and towards her.
- Nobody else. No sister, no friend, no message thread. The film has one voice in it.

## The hook

Frame 1 is the lock screen: `23:12`, and a notification that reads **Home — You arrived at
23:11**. She is not home, and the first line of narration says so before the viewer has finished
reading the notification. Situation, contradiction and question in one image and one sentence.

No caption repeats a line of narration. The device sheet is switched off in the two map scenes
where it would cover her dot, so the pins carry those beats on their own.

## Rules this film keeps

- **Frame 1 is the impossible image.** No logo, no intro card, no "story time".
- **The software is honest.** Every number is a number Find My would really print. The phone is
  never wrong; that is what makes it frightening.
- **Nothing explains the anomaly.** The narration states facts. It never theorises.
- **The camera is motivated.** She has it because of a break-in, said once, in the line that also
  tells the viewer what they are looking at.
- **No sequel hook.** The last line is a place, not a promise.

## What changed from the 60 s cut, and why

| 28-km-60s | Arrived | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A sister, a message thread, a name resolving on a pin | Nothing but her, her phone and her hall | The other characters were only ever text. Removing them removed the need to explain them. |
| Narration describing a night walked "backwards" | Narration naming the object in front of the camera | The old lines were true to the script and false to the picture. |
| Twists stacked: the pin becomes ASHA, her dot disappears, the phone relights | One turn: it arrives, and the hall it came from is empty | Stacked reveals read as a puzzle. One reveal, paid for by four minutes of the same night, reads as a threat. |
| 27 s of abstract map over 60 s | 20 s of map, all of it moving towards her | The map is evidence, not wallpaper. |
