# Topic backlog

The editorial backlog for the video studio. One record per serious candidate.

**Selection rule:** pick the highest-scoring topic with status `shortlisted`. Do not pick by checkbox order.
**Voice-over:** every reel is narrated (`docs/02-story-and-writing.md`). A topic that cannot carry a
first-person spoken line inside the hook window is not eligible, however good the image is.
**Lifecycle (one state only):** `idea → shortlisted → scripted → in-production → rendered → reviewed → published → measured`
**Scoring (§4.2):** 1–5 on hook clarity, curiosity, payoff potential, originality, visual readability, participation, production feasibility — weighted 20/20/20/15/10/10/5 to a score out of 100. `80+` shortlist · `65–79` improve the premise · `<65` hold or retire.
**Nothing in this repository has been published.** No record here has a post URL, a publication date, or a metric. Per §4.4 a topic reaches `published` only when a human confirms the actual post.

## DS-002 — 2.8 km

- **Pillar:** Location and navigation
- **Premise:** Her phone is in her hand, but Find My shows a second device labelled "Me" 2.8 km away, and it is moving toward her.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Paranoia; pursuit by proxy.
- **Hook (frame 1):** Find My map. Her blue dot at centre, a grey dot labelled **Me** on the ring road, `2.8 km`.
- **Central question:** Whose phone is walking toward her, and what happens when it arrives?
- **Escalation:** `2.8 km → 1.1 km → 400 m` while she does not move · `Me arrived at your location` · the map zooms to floor level, 3 m behind her.
- **Reveal / payoff:** The grey label changes from "Me" to her full name, then to a dot with no name — and her own blue dot disappears. She is no longer on the map.
- **Ending type:** Closed twist, with one deliberately unanswered question.
- **Participation angle:** Prediction — "how close before you'd stop looking?" Natural because the distance is the story.
- **Series link:** Standalone.
- **Freshness note:** Location engine. No messages, no calls, no door. The antagonist is a dot that only moves on a screen.
- **Production needs:** **`map` scene type** — built for this film and reused by DS-006 and DS-009. Audio: room tone, seven proximity pings rising in pitch, one notification chime, one dropout, real silence at the end. No voice-over.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Cover = the map with the two dots 3 m apart; text under six words.
- **Status:** `rendered` — `projects/28-km-60s`, 1800 frames / 60.00 s, verify 11/11, determinism identical across 30 sampled frames. Narrated by nine takes of one voice; the voice was auditioned before use, but **the finished mix has not been listened to end to end by a human**.
- **Results:** None. Not published.

**Rebuild (60 s, narrated).** The 30 s cut was a puzzle with nobody in it, so DS-002 was rebuilt rather than patched: same map, same rule, and a woman telling you what it means. Nine voice-over lines (104 words) leave deliberate silences at 16.8–22.0 s and 41.8–46.6 s. Eight scenes, ending on a lock screen that lights itself. **This film runs 60 s while batch 1 is specified at 30 s** — a documented exception: the story needs the room, and the 30 s silent cut stays in the repo as the comparison. It is therefore *not* a clean length experiment: length, narration and structure all change at once.

**Build notes (30 s cut).** Three scenes, not the four in the brief: the arrival moment became a banner over the map rather than a lock screen, because the phone is in her hand and a lock screen would contradict what the viewer can see. The film has no end card — it closes on the map's own empty state, which is the first time the catalogue has ended without a two-line card. The banner reads `Started sharing location`, not `Arrived at your location`: at 400 m the app would be lying, and the one rule this film cannot break is that the software is always correct.

**Score 94** — hook 5 · curiosity 5 · payoff 4 · originality 5 · visual 5 · participation 4 · feasibility 5 → `20+20+16+15+10+8+5`.

## DS-001 — New Memory

- **Pillar:** Digital memory
- **Premise:** Photos resurfaces a "1 year ago today" memory of her bedroom taken from a corner she does not have — in a flat that has been locked all week.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Intrusion; slow dread; domestic unease.
- **Hook (frame 1):** A full-screen photo of her own bedroom from a slightly wrong angle; the caption reads `1 year ago today`.
- **Central question:** Who is taking these — and why does the room keep getting tidier?
- **Escalation:** It is not one photo but twelve, same corner, seven nights · each one tidier than the last · the metadata says tonight, not a year ago.
- **Reveal / payoff:** The last photo, three minutes old, shows her bed with a second pillow. The auto-caption reads *"1 year ago today — with one other person."*
- **Ending type:** Closed twist.
- **Participation angle:** Evidence spotting — "watch the room, not the phone": count what moved between photos.
- **Series link:** Standalone. Shared-universe detail available: the second pillow.
- **Freshness note:** The horror is housekeeping, not a person. Deliberately **not** another photo of the protagonist (DOD already spent that image). No messages, no door.
- **Production needs:** `photo-viewer` (existing) plus a gallery-grid variant; captions; audio: room tone, one shutter click per photo arrival, no voice.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Cover = the wide bedroom photo with the second pillow visible.
- **Status:** `shortlisted`
- **Results:** None. Not published.

**Score 95** — 5 · 5 · 5 · 4 · 5 · 4 · 5.

## DS-003 — 9:00 PM

- **Pillar:** Time and prediction
- **Premise:** A calendar event she did not create appears tonight at 9:00 PM — "Last chance to leave" — added by her own account, located at her home address.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Anticipation; deadline dread.
- **Hook (frame 1):** Day view, the 9 PM block filled in red, `LAST CHANCE TO LEAVE`.
- **Central question:** What happens at 9:00, and who is keeping the appointment?
- **Escalation:** `Event added by you` · she deletes it and it returns with a countdown · tapping the location opens Maps: 0 minutes away · she leaves anyway.
- **Reveal / payoff:** At 9:00 PM, outside, the notification reads `You left something behind` — and every entry for tomorrow has been replaced by one word: `return`.
- **Ending type:** Consequential reveal.
- **Participation angle:** Prediction — "what's at 9:00?" Set up honestly: the event has a title, a place, and no description.
- **Series link:** Standalone. Continuity note: 9:00 PM is also the knock deadline in the voicemail series — do not present this as the same world.
- **Freshness note:** Nobody sends her anything. The device is the antagonist.
- **Production needs:** A `calendar` surface (day grid, event sheet, countdown) — new, and used by nothing else, so it carries its full cost in one film.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Cover = the red 9:00 block with the rest of the day blurred.
- **Status:** `shortlisted`
- **Results:** None. Not published.

**Score 95** — 5 · 5 · 5 · 4 · 5 · 4 · 5.

## DS-004 — You were added to a group

- **Pillar:** Messages from the impossible
- **Premise:** She is added to a group of seven people she knows. None of them added her. It has been running for six years.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Collective dread; being talked about.
- **Hook (frame 1):** `You were added to a group` above a member strip of seven faces — one circle blank.
- **Central question:** Who made this group, and why is all of it about her?
- **Escalation:** Six years of history, all about her, dated before she owned this phone · a reply comes from a disconnected number · members leave one at a time and the count stops at two.
- **Reveal / payoff:** The last message is hers — `don't add me yet` — timestamped six years ago. She types `?`. It is already there.
- **Ending type:** Consequential reveal.
- **Participation angle:** Tactical — "who would you ask first?" Backed by evidence: one member never leaves.
- **Series link:** Standalone.
- **Freshness note:** An archive, not an incoming call. Reuses the thread surface but not the own-number device, and there is no door.
- **Production needs:** `message-thread` extension (member strip, system rows, leave events). Audio: one notification per departure, no voice.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Cover = the member strip with the blank circle.
- **Status:** `shortlisted`
- **Results:** None. Not published.

## DS-007 — Last seen 4 years ago

## DS-013 — The Inventory (2 × 30 s)

- **Pillar:** Connected-system mystery
- **Premise:** A shared document she does not remember creating is typing an inventory of her flat, in the right order, down to the chipped mug.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Domestic uncanny; being catalogued.
- **Hook (frame 1):** A document mid-sentence, character by character: `ONE (1) MUG, CHIPPED, LEFT OF SINK`.
- **Central question:** Who is counting her things, and what happens when the list ends?
- **Escalation (part 1):** 214 items, dated nightly · the typing stops when she puts the phone down and resumes when she picks it up · item 215: `ONE (1) PERSON, WAITING, NOT LISTED`.
- **Escalation (part 2):** The file reopens as `THINGS I TOOK`; her possessions are struck out one at a time; the last strike-through is `the phone you're holding`; 0.4 s of black.
- **Reveal / payoff:** Part 1 — the document is renamed `FINAL` and the cursor keeps counting. Part 2 — it returns titled `RETURNED`, and the count is one higher than before.
- **Ending type:** Part 1 consequential reveal; part 2 ambiguous loop.
- **Participation angle:** Part 1 evidence spotting ("one item on this list is not in your flat"); part 2 continuity.
- **Series link:** Mini-series, level 2 — two 30 s parts. Both must work alone; part 2 opens with a three-second reminder of the document.
- **Freshness note:** Collaborative editing as the horror surface. No messages, no calls, no doors, no photographs.
- **Production needs:** A `doc-editor` scene type (document surface, live cursor, strikethrough rows) — one new type, amortised over two films.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Part 1 cover = the highlighted line `ONE (1) PERSON`; part 2 = a page of struck-through items.
- **Status:** `shortlisted`
- **Results:** None. Not published.

**Score 97** — 5 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 4 · 4.

## DS-014 — Two versions of 8:41

- **Pillar:** Audience-choice experiment
- **Premise:** Her screenshots folder holds two versions of the same conversation, four minutes apart, and only one of them can be real.
- **Subgenre / emotion:** Cognitive dread; unreliable memory.
- **Hook (frame 1):** A screenshot of a chat with her mother — and a badge on the card that reads `2 versions`.
- **Central question:** Which conversation actually happened?
- **Escalation:** The same thread at 23:41 and 23:45 · one says `who's with you?`, the other `there's nobody at the door` · the screenshot log says both were taken by her, at 02:13.
- **Reveal / payoff:** Screen-time shows the gallery was closed 02:13–02:17; the only activity in those four minutes is the screenshot itself. Both versions end on the same line from her mother — `are you awake` — at 02:14.
- **Ending type:** Ambiguous / audience interpretation.
- **Participation angle:** Interpretation, stated plainly in the caption — "which one do you remember?" Both readings stay supported.
- **Series link:** Standalone. **Not** the voicemail world — do not reuse its times or devices.
- **Freshness note:** Complete evidence, no answer. This is the catalogue's first non-bridge ending; the ambiguity is the product, not a hedge.
- **Production needs:** `photo-viewer` for the two screenshot cards, plus a gallery grid. No new scene type if the grid is a variant.
- **Platform packaging idea:** Cover = two identical thumbnails, one word different.
- **Status:** `shortlisted`
- **Results:** None. Not published.

**Score 95** — 5 · 5 · 4 · 5 · 5 · 5 · 4.
