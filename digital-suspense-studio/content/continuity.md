# Continuity ledger

Facts established on screen. A new film may reuse a fact only if it does not contradict this file. All names, numbers, accounts and addresses are invented.

---

## World A — the voicemail series (parts 1–4)

**Accounts and identifiers**
- The voicemail arrives from `Me` — the protagonist's own number. Seen in parts 1, 2 and 4; referenced in `dont-open-the-door`.
- The protagonist is only ever named once, in the 43 s cut of part 1: **Sarah**, spoken by the voicemail. The 30 s cut does not use the name. She has no surname and no visible face.
- No other character is named or shown. Voices are synthetic.

**Times and dates**
- Part 1 voicemail is received **tomorrow, 8:14 AM**; the lock screen reads **8:47 PM**, Thursday 12 June.
- The deadline in the series is **9:00 PM, three knocks**. Part 1 announces it; part 2 receives the call at 8:48 PM; part 4 ends at 8:47 PM.
- `dont-open-the-door` runs on **8:41 PM** with a missed call at 8:48 PM. Same evening, different flat — treat as a sibling story, not a chapter.

**Locations**
- Her flat: hallway with five doors, front door at the far end, one door that is open when it should be shut.
- The bridge is mentioned once in the 43 s cut and never shown.

**Rules the world obeys**
- The phone receives a voicemail recorded a day in the future by the protagonist herself.
- Part 4's final frame is byte-identical to part 1's opening frame — the loop is enforced by a test, not by copy.
- Every film carries the `FICTIONAL STORY` label on every scene.

**Questions answered**
- What the voicemail says. Who is calling in part 2 (her own number). What is inside the flat in part 3 (a figure at the far end of the hall). Who recorded part 4's voicemail (her).

**Questions deliberately unresolved**
- What is on the other end of the call, and why it uses her number.
- What happens at 9:00 PM if the door is not opened.
- Whether the loop has run before.

**Contradictions to avoid**
- Do not give her a surname, a face, or a second speaking character.
- Do not reschedule the 9:00 PM knock.
- Do not reuse the `would you …?` end-card question (retired after part 3).
- Do not label a new film `PART 5` unless it returns to this same world.

## World B — the standalone batch-1 films

Independent stories that may share a surface, never a plot. A viewer must never need one to understand another.

**Protagonist**
- First name only: **Asha**. Named on screen for the first time in DS-002, on a map pin.
  No surname, no face, no age. Other World B films may leave her unnamed; nothing may
  contradict the name.

**Recurring surfaces (allowed, no continuity implied)**
- `map` — DS-002, DS-006, DS-009. Same app chrome, different addresses.
- `photo-viewer` — DS-001, DS-014.
- The flat with the second pillow (DS-001) may reappear as set dressing only.

**Recurring details already agreed**
- **9:00 PM** is the calendar deadline in DS-003 and the arrival time in DS-006 (`arrive 9:04`). Quiet callback; never explained.
- **02:13–02:17** is the four-minute hole in DS-014. Do not reuse it as a timestamp anywhere else.
- **`ONE (1) PERSON, WAITING, NOT LISTED`** is the last line of DS-013 part 1 and the first thing struck through in part 2.

**Contradictions to avoid**
- Do not imply World A and World B are the same flat. Different furniture, different windows, different doors.
- Do not put the protagonist of World A into a World B film.
- Do not let two films in batch 1 use the same ending type back to back.

## Packaging constants

- Brand line: **Your phone knows something it shouldn't.** Used in captions, not on screen.
- Every scene carries `FICTIONAL STORY`.
- Captions are a separate track: never a transcript of the voice-over, never more than five words, one idea per card, capitals reserved for the threat word.
- Every caption lands 0.3–0.5 s before the sound it belongs to.
- Closed twist, consequential reveal, ambiguous, sequel bridge and audience interpretation are all allowed; no two consecutive films may end the same way.
