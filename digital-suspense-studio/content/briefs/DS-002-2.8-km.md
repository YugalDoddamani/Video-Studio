- **Topic ID:** DS-002
- **Pillar:** Location and navigation
- **Score:** 94 (hook 5 · curiosity 5 · payoff 4 · originality 5 · visual 5 · participation 4 · feasibility 5)
- **Runtime:** 30.0 s — 900 frames at 30 fps (batch standard)

---

## Premise

Her phone is in her hand, but Find My shows a second device labelled **Me** 2.8 km away — and it is moving toward her.

## Why it is meaningfully different

Every existing film is a conversation: someone calls, texts, or records. This one has no sender, no message, and no voice. The only thing that moves is a dot, and the only thing the viewer has to do is watch the distance fall. It also introduces the location pillar, which the catalogue has never touched, and it is the first film that does not open on a person's screen *content* but on a map — a surface with no author.

## The one-line rule

The map is honest and the world is not. Nothing on screen malfunctions. The app is correct the whole time.

## Sound design (no voice)

| Element | Where | Treatment | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Room tone | 0 → 28.4 s | 52 Hz + 104 Hz hum, brown noise lowpassed at 260 Hz | −46 dB mean |
| Proximity ping ×3 | 1.0 s, 4.6 s, 7.8 s | 1180 Hz sine, 60 ms, exponential decay, plus a 70 Hz sub-thump | −18 dB peak |
| Ping intervals | — | 3.6 s → 3.2 s → then stop at the cut | — |
| Arrival chime | 10.2 s | two-tone 1046 + 1568 Hz, 0.55 s decay, second tone 40 ms late | −12 dB peak |
| Floor-level tick ×2 | 17.4 s, 25.6 s | 60 ms filtered noise, wood-ish | −24 dB peak |
| Digital silence | 28.4 → 30.0 s | true silence, nothing normalised into it | −91 dB |

No music. The ping is the score. Every element is synthesised with `lavfi` sources — no external assets, no new dependencies.

## The `map` scene type

**Why:** no combination of existing types can render a map. Three shortlisted topics need it (DS-002, DS-006, DS-009), so the cost is amortised across the batch.

**Composition (all CSS/SVG, no tiles, no network):**
- `mapCanvas` — dark canvas, seeded street grid (mulberry32 on a fixed seed so the same frame always renders the same pixels), one park polygon, one river polygon, a scale bar, a `Recents`/`Devices` segmented control at the top with `Devices` selected.
- `mapPin` — blue dot with a 1.9 s pulse ring (hers), grey pin with a label chip (the other).
- `mapSheet` — bottom card: device name, model line, distance, `Last updated`.
- Optional `zoom` prop that scales the grid and re-centres, so scenes 1 and 3 are the same component at two scales.

**Scene data:** `{ statusTime, zoom, centre, distance, distanceAt, pins[], sheet, empty }`.

**Feasibility note:** the whole surface is static markup with frame-driven transforms, which is what the renderer is fastest at. Expect 8–10 minutes for 900 frames like the other films.

## Packaging

- **Title:** `2.8 km` (alternate: `Find My shows a second you`)
- **Cover:** the frame at f630 — two dots, the name chip reading ASHA, `3 m` between them. Cover text under six words: `one of these is you`.
- **Caption:** hook line + one question + disclosure.
- **Primary CTA:** prediction — *"how close before you'd stop looking?"* One question, asked once.

## Learning hypothesis (written before posting)

A film with no voice and no character on screen will hold completion at least as well as the four voiced films, because the only moving object on screen is the thing the viewer is already looking at. If completion is materially lower, the cause is likely the absence of a human voice rather than the map surface, and the next location film (DS-006) should be tested with a voice note before that conclusion is drawn.
