## Why this cut exists

The 30 s version was a puzzle with no person in it. It showed a dot closing on another dot and
asked the viewer to be frightened by arithmetic. Nothing said who she was, where she was
going, or what she would lose — so the impossibility arrived before the stakes, and a stranger
scrolling past had no reason to care.

This cut keeps the map and puts a life underneath it. Same night, same app, same rule that the
software is never wrong — but now a woman tells you, in her own voice, what it means.

---

## The hook, and where it comes from

The first three seconds carry a situation, an impossibility and a question, in one spoken
sentence, over one image. The line is:

> **"My phone says I'm two point eight kilometres away. I'm holding it."**

That sentence is built from four techniques that recur across thriller and short-form craft:

| Technique | What it does here | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrive late / in medias res** | No waking up, no commute, no establishing shot. We arrive with the phone already in her hand and the impossible reading already on screen. | [character.app — cold opens](https://www.character.app/guides/how-to-write-a-cold-open-hook) · [thriller.today](https://thriller.today/how-to-write-thriller-openings/) |
| **Withhold the explanation** | The line states the fact and the contradiction; it never explains the app, the device, or why. Context is the reward for watching, not the price of admission. | [character.app](https://www.character.app/guides/how-to-write-a-cold-open-hook) |
| **Violation of normality** | A phone is the most normal object in the frame. The violation is a single number attached to it. | [thriller.today](https://thriller.today/how-to-write-thriller-openings/) |
| **The knowledge gap** | By second twelve she knows there is a second *her* on the map. The viewer knows what her sister does not — and then learns what she does not. | [thriller.today](https://thriller.today/how-to-write-thriller-openings/) · [authorversity](https://www.authorversity.com/how-to-write-a-thriller/) |

Two more from the same references shape the rest: a **ticking clock** (the distance is a
countdown printed on screen, so the reel has a clock even though nobody sets one), and an
opening that ends on a **shift in power** (she kills the phone; the phone comes back).

Horror-specific craft — anchor in realism before the fear, reveal the threat slowly, use
silence as punctuation — is from [Final Draft's horror-opening guide](https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/how-to-write-a-horror-screenplay-opening-that-hooks-readers-).

## Premise

Asha, a night nurse, walks home at eleven. Tonight Find My lists a second device called **Me**,
2.8 km from her, and it is retracing her walk home in reverse — the hospital, the bus stop, her
street. It is not chasing her. It is repeating her.

## Characters

- **Asha**, 34 — paediatric nurse, six years on the same walk home. Never seen; only heard.
- **Meera**, her sister — never seen, never heard. Present only as a text.
- The device called **Me** — a phone. It walks her night backwards.

## Story structure

### Hook (0–5 s)

Map. Her blue dot at centre. A grey pin marked **Me**, 2.8 km up the ring road.
**"My phone says I'm two point eight kilometres away. I'm holding it."**

### Setup (5–11 s)

A wet street at night, headlights, the sound of walking. **"I'm a nurse. I finish at eleven.
I walk home the same way every night."** Now the viewer knows who she is and why she is alone.

### Escalation (11–35 s)

- **"Tonight there's a second phone on the map. It's called Me."**
- **"It's coming up the ring road. Toward my street."**
- **"I tap it. It shows where it's been."** The sheet expands: `Hospital 23:11`, `Bus stop 23:19`, `Ring road now`.
- **"The hospital, at eleven eleven. The bus stop, at eleven nineteen."**
- **"It's walking my night. Backwards."** — the turn: it is not following her, it is *being* her.

### Twist (35–48 s)

**"I ring my sister. She lives two streets over."**
Her sister texts back: *"you're at my door. i just let you in."*
**"I'm not at her door. My phone is still in my hand."**
Someone wearing her location is inside her sister's flat.

### Climax (48–56 s)

The map closes: 400 m → 3 m. **"Three metres."** The pin resolves from **Me** to **ASHA**.
**"Asha."** Her own dot leaves the map.

### Ending (56–60 s)

**"So I turn it off."** The screen dies.
Black. Then the lock screen lights itself: `Me — 0 m`.
**"It comes back on by itself."**

## Sound

- Voice-over: pitch down 2.5 semitones, band-limited to a phone-line range, 40 ms ghost, measured
  to −21 dB mean — the same chain as the voicemail series, so the channel sounds like itself.
- Room tone under everything; seven proximity pings rising in pitch as the distance falls; one
  chime at the sister's reply; a hard dropout when the phone is switched off; true silence after.
- Streaming app compression is the enemy of the last two seconds: the tail stays dead quiet.
